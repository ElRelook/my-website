Start-up project · G2Elab · 2026

CaStore, project co-lead

CaStore is a sovereign platform for collecting, structuring and predictively analysing energy data: electricity, gas and water. I lead the project with Thomas Calmant; I am in charge of data analysis, forecasting and user experience.

The problem. The power grid is under increasing strain, and the electrification of everyday uses will only add to it. An energy community installing solar panels needs precise forecasts of its consumption and production to maximise self-consumption. A social housing provider is more interested in spotting anomalies: an appliance using too much power, a water leak. They all face the same limits: dependence on a single provider, poorly interoperable data and badly automated data collection.

Our answer. CaStore brings data from very different sources (meters, comfort sensors, weather, solar production, batteries) into one common structure, whatever the sensor type. An analysis layer then turns this raw data into concrete services:

VisibilityConsumption and production in an interface co-designed with its users.
PredictabilityConsumption and production forecasts to support decisions.
SufficiencyUnderstand and reduce consumption, detect drifts and anomalies.
FlexibilityShift usage and control equipment to relieve the grid.
SovereigntyData hosted in France; each owner controls who accesses their data and how it is used.

The platform is designed from the start for GDPR, the Data Act, the AI Act and NIS2. It targets energy communities, social housing providers and energy suppliers, and is built to handle more than 100,000 sensors.

CORYS builds training and engineering simulators for the energy and rail sectors. In the R&D team of the Energy division, I replaced Excel calculation spreadsheets with Python tools that are easier to maintain.
